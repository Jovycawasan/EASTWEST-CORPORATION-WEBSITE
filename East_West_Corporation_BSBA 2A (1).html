Background & History

History

EastWest Bank was established on July 6, 1994, following the liberalization of the Philippine banking industry. It commenced operations on August 1, 1994, with its first branch located along Sen. Gil Puyat Avenue in Makati City. The bank's name reflects its aim to blend the traditional values of the East with the efficiency of the West.

Over the years, EastWest Bank has expanded its services through strategic acquisitions, including Ecology Savings Bank in 2002, AIG Philam Savings Bank in 2009, and the retail banking business of Standard Chartered Bank Philippines in 2016. In 2020, it launched "Komo," a fully digital banking service under its subsidiary, EastWest Rural Bank, Inc.

Corporate Profile

🏢 EastWest Bank at a Glance

East West Banking Corporation, known as EastWest Bank, is one of the fastest-growing banks in the Philippines. It was incorporated on July 6, 1994 and started operations on August 1, 1994. It is a subsidiary of Filinvest Development Corporation, a large Philippine conglomerate with interests in real estate, hospitality, power generation, and sugar.

EastWest Bank is publicly listed on the Philippine Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol EW and operates as a universal bank, offering a full range of financial products and services to consumers, businesses, and corporations.

Subsidiaries and Affiliates

  • EastWest Rural Bank, Inc.
    – A wholly owned rural banking arm that enables financial inclusion in rural areas and supports EastWest's digital bank product, Komo.
  • EastWest Ageas Life Insurance Corporation

  • EastWest Insurance Brokerage, Inc.
    – Offers non-life insurance products such as auto, fire, accident, and marine insurance.
  • EastWest Leasing and Finance Corporation
    – Provides financing and leasing services for equipment, vehicles, and other assets.
  • Assurance Solutions Insurance Agency, Inc.
    – Acts as an insurance agency partner to support distribution of various insurance products.
  • Quest Marketing and Integrated Services, Inc.
    – Handles sales and marketing activities for various EastWest products and services.

Scope of Business/Operation

EastWest Bank operates as a universal bank in the Philippines, meaning it is authorized to offer a complete range of commercial, investment, and consumer banking services. Its operations span across various financial sectors, serving individuals, corporations, and small businesses nationwide.

  • Deposits: Savings, checking, and time deposit accounts
  • Consumer Loans: Auto loans, personal loans, salary loans, and housing loans
  • Credit Cards: EastWest credit card offerings for retail clients

  • Insurance and Bancassurance: Life and non-life insurance products via EastWest Ageas and partner providers

  • Corporate Loans: Short-term and long-term lending solutions

  • Fixed income securities

Organizational Structure

The organizational structure of EastWest Bank is designed to support its diverse range of banking products and services, from retail banking to corporate and investment banking. It features a hierarchical structure that includes senior management, various divisions, and departments to oversee operations, risk management, and customer services.

1. Board of Directors

The Board of Directors provides oversight, strategic direction, and corporate governance for the bank. Key members include the Chairperson, Vice Chairperson, and independent and non-independent directors.

2. Executive Management

  • President and CEO: Responsible for overall strategy, operations, and performance of the bank.
  • Chief Operating Officer (COO): Handles day-to-day operations and ensures the smooth running of all banking functions.
  • Chief Financial Officer (CFO): Oversees financial planning, management, and risk assessments.
  • Chief Risk Officer (CRO): Ensures risk management strategies are effectively implemented.
  • Chief Compliance Officer (CCO): Focuses on regulatory compliance and internal controls.

3. Business Units / Divisions

EastWest Bank is divided into several key divisions that focus on different aspects of banking:

Retail Banking Division

  • Personal Banking: Manages individual customers, offering savings accounts, loans, and credit cards.
  • Digital Banking: Focuses on mobile and online banking services, including the Komo digital bank.
  • Customer Service and Relationship Management: Handles inquiries, complaints, and relationship management for retail clients.

Corporate Banking Division

  • Corporate Loans: Provides financing for businesses, including working capital and term loans.
  • Trade Finance: Manages international trade, letters of credit, and other trade services.
  • Treasury: Focuses on managing liquidity, foreign exchange, and investment activities for corporate clients.

Commercial Banking Division

  • Small and Medium Enterprise (SME) Banking: Supports small to medium-sized businesses with loans and banking services tailored to their needs.
  • Business Banking: Provides a range of financial products for growing businesses.

Investment and Wealth Management Division

  • Trust Services: Manages investment accounts, unit investment trust funds (UITFs), and retirement funds.
  • Private Banking: Provides personalized financial planning and wealth management for high-net-worth clients.

Risk and Compliance Division

  • Risk Management: Identifies and manages various risks (credit, market, operational, etc.).
  • Compliance: Ensures the bank follows regulatory requirements and internal controls.

4. Support Functions and Departments

  • Finance Department: Handles accounting, financial reporting, and budget management.
  • Human Resources: Manages recruitment, employee relations, compensation, and training.
  • Marketing and Communications: Responsible for branding, advertising, public relations, and customer engagement.
  • Legal Department: Provides legal advice, manages contracts, and ensures legal compliance.
  • Operations and IT: Manages bank operations and ensures technology infrastructure supports the bank's needs.
  • Audit: Conducts internal audits to ensure compliance with policies and procedures.

5. Subsidiaries and Affiliates

EastWest Bank has various subsidiaries that extend its services:

  • EastWest Rural Bank: Focuses on serving rural areas with financial services tailored to the local economy.
  • EastWest Leasing & Finance Corporation: Provides leasing and financing products.
  • EastWest Ageas Life Insurance Company: Joint venture with Ageas Insurance offering life insurance products.
  • Komo by EastWest: Digital banking platform offered by EastWest Rural Bank.

Corporate Social Responsibility

EastWest Bank is committed to corporate social responsibility (CSR) through various initiatives that support community development, promote financial literacy, and address pressing social issues. Below are five notable CSR programs:

EastWest Bank launched the Magenta Race, a virtual event aimed at raising funds for breast cancer detection and prevention. In its inaugural year, the initiative garnered significant participation from employees and the public, raising funds for the Philippine Foundation for Breast Care Inc. Key Outcomes:

  • Increased access to affordable housing for low-income families
  • Support for transitional housing facilities

In partnership with nonprofit organizations, EastWest Bank has implemented financial literacy programs aimed at empowering underserved communities.
